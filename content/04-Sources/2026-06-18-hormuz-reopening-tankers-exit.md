# Strait of Hormuz Begins Reopening as Tankers Exit US Blockade

## Summary
At least three Iranian tankers carrying ~5 million barrels of crude exited the US Navy blockade in the Strait of Hormuz — the first outbound shipment in roughly two months — as shipowners reposition ahead of the Geneva MOU signing. The pact is expected to reopen Hormuz and waive sanctions on Iranian oil. Shipowners remain cautious, awaiting safety assurances before resuming transits.

## Key Data Points
- Pre-war Hormuz traffic: ~3,000 vessels/month; ~15M bpd of oil (≈1/5 of world oil trade)
- During war: collapsed >90%; only 191 vessels recorded crossing in all of April 2026
- Closure duration: ~Feb 28/Mar 4 – mid-June 2026 (~3.5 months), now reversing
- June 17: 3 Iranian tankers (~5M bbl) exit blockade, first outbound in ~2 months
- Trump lifted US naval blockade of ships docked at Iranian ports
- Shipowners "cautiously awaiting more details" — reopening is gradual, not instant

## Bias Assessment
CNBC and Al Jazeera are mainstream outlets with corroborating maritime data; UANI (United Against Nuclear Iran) is an advocacy group but its shipping-tracking data is widely cited and quantitative. Event facts (tankers exiting, blockade lifted) are consistent across sources.
