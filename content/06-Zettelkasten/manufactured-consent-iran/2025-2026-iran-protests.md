# The 2025–2026 Iran Protests

**Core idea:** A nationwide economic uprising that began on 28 December 2025 in Tehran's Grand Bazaar — triggered by the collapse of the rial — which escalated into the political crisis that preceded and overlapped the [[CLAIM-001-us-iran-war|US/Israel–Iran war]].

## The Economic Trigger

The protests began on [28 December 2025 inside the markets of Tehran](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/iran-country-policy-and-information-notes/country-bulletin-iran-protests-of-december-2025-to-january-2026-accessible) and spread within days to Isfahan, Shiraz, and Mashhad. The proximate cause was the [collapse of the rial](https://www.cnn.com/2025/12/29/middleeast/iran-protests-currency-low-latam-intl), which fell to a record low of roughly **1.42–1.5 million rial to the dollar (~138,000–145,000 toman)** — against 430,000 in 2022 and just 32,000 at the time of the 2015 nuclear deal. The head of the Central Bank resigned. This was the [largest unrest since 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_Iranian_protests).

This is the economic-collapse trigger Professor Jiang's framework anticipates: empire decline and economic strangulation producing internal instability, which external actors then exploit. See [[law-of-asymmetry]].

## The Slogans

The chants varied. The slogan that drew the most international attention was **"Javid Shah"** ("Long live the King"), which tracked the rising profile of the exiled [[reza-pahlavi-position-shift|Reza Pahlavi]]. That this monarchist framing rose so quickly is itself relevant — it is precisely the narrative that the [[israeli-influence-operation-iran|documented Israeli influence operation]] had been amplifying since before the protests began.

## From Economic to Political

What started as bread-and-currency protest was rapidly reframed as a regime-change moment. The escalation accelerated after Pahlavi's [8–9 January call to action](https://www.euronews.com/2026/01/08/mass-protests-erupt-in-irans-capital-after-exiled-princes-call-internet-shut-off-nationwid), after which the state imposed the [[iran-internet-blackout|internet blackout]] — and from that point the factual record fractures into competing casualty narratives.

> **Discipline note:** Turnout figures of "1.5 million in Tehran / 5 million nationwide" trace to a single unnamed European diplomat, not an independent count. They should be treated with the same skepticism this cluster applies to the [[casualty-inflation-narrative|inflated death toll]].
